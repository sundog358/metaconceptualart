About this reconstruction

Read this before citing anything here.

 

What this is

A reconstruction, made in 2026, of the website Sun & Rain Works says it built in 2009 at www.wtfisart.com. It was assembled from two things the studio still holds: the WTFIA folder of source documents made for that site, and a second, unrelated 2009 website, Growing Aloha, built by the same hands in the same months, which survives complete with its HTML, its stylesheet and its Dreamweaver template.

The content is the WTFIA folder's. The form is Growing Aloha's, copied closely: XHTML 1.0 Transitional, a Dreamweaver "three column liquid, header and footer" layout, the inline style block wrapped in HTML comments, the Internet Explorer conditional comment, the block-link navigation column, and a stylesheet of content-named classes. Where this site had to guess, it guessed by looking at what the sibling site did.

 

What this is not

It is not a recovered copy. No HTML from www.wtfisart.com survives in the studio's archive, and none is known to exist anywhere. The evidence record for the WTFIA folder states this plainly: the folder holds what was made for the site, not what the site published.

It is not evidence that the site went live in 2009. The earliest capture of wtfisart.com in the Internet Archive, from January 2010, is a redirect from a hosting account that was serving no content, and the next, from February 2011, is a parked advertising page — positive evidence that the site was not serving content at either date. When it was live, if it was, is not established.

The layout is inference, not record. Nothing in the surviving material shows how the 2009 pages were arranged, what they were called, or what the site's front page said. The page titles, the navigation order, the home-page wording and the division into sections are this reconstruction's choices, made to fit the documents to the sibling site's template.

 

What is actually from 2009

The text. Every gallery name, web address, headline, byline, date and quotation on these pages is transcribed from the four Word documents in the WTFIA folder, whose embedded metadata dates them to January and March 2009:

· IsItArt.doc — 24 dated press reports on the art market, 2000 to 2009, 22 of them with the source link recorded. Fourteen pages, about four thousand words. Created 22 January 2009, last saved 28 January 2009. Its Title metadata field reads "August 13, 2007", which is simply one of the dated entries inside it, not a date of authorship.

· OldMasters.doc — 64 dealers and galleries, every one with its web address. Created 23 January 2009, last saved 26 January 2009.

· Modern.doc — 81 modern and contemporary galleries, grouped by state and country, with no web addresses. Created 27 January 2009, last saved 28 January 2009; produced with a different word processor from the other three.

· Photographers.doc — a one-page note on the photographer Lee Balterman. Created 22 January 2009.

A second contributor is named in the metadata of all four documents, as author of both gallery directories and as last editor of every file. That person's name is withheld pending their consent. The two directories are their work, not the studio principal's.

 

What was deliberately left out

· The advertising. Growing Aloha carried a Google Custom Search box and three AdSense units, and the surviving code contains the studio's own publisher and partner identifiers. Live third-party script and personal account identifiers are not reproduced; the slots are marked where they sat.

· The images. The WTFIA folder holds art-historical plates and art-market press pictures gathered in 2009, all of them third-party material held back pending rights review. The one photograph shown here, the verso of the Ansel Adams print, is the studio's own and was already published.

· The storage-unit photographs. The 2009 session documenting the Adams print shows an identifiable person and is withheld pending consent.

· The tiled background photograph Growing Aloha used, which is a personal photograph from an unrelated project.

 

On the quotations

The excerpts on the Is It Art? page are short passages from copyrighted news reports, transcribed from the 2009 compilation and reproduced here for commentary, each one attributed to its author and publication and linked to its source. Where the compiler recorded no link, that is said so on the page.

 

On the pairing with the auction

Sun & Rain Works describes www.wtfisart.com and the 2009 Sotheby's consignment as companion works meant to go together. That is the artist's account and is recorded as such. The dates are consistent with it — the latest material in the folder was last saved a week before the sale — but consistency is not proof of intent, and no document in the folder mentions the consignment.
